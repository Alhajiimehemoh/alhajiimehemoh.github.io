Survey123 form mistakes to avoid

Common problems that make field data messy, and the simple form design choices that prevent them.

21 October 2026 · Data collection · 1 min read

Most data quality problems start in the form, not in the spreadsheet. Fixing the form is cheaper than cleaning the data every month.

Free-text where a list would do

If facility names are typed, you will get five spellings of the same clinic. Use a choice list so every record uses the same name.

No limits on numbers

A field for children vaccinated should not accept 5,000. Add a constraint with a clear message so the mistake is caught on the phone, while the health worker is still at the facility.

Too many questions

Every extra question lowers the chance that the form is filled in properly. Ask only what you will use in a report. If nobody will read an answer, remove the question.

Asking for names you do not need

Collect the least personal information possible. Patient names and phone numbers create risk and are often not needed for programme reporting.

Skipping skip logic

If a follow-up question only applies when the answer is yes, show it only then. This keeps the form short and avoids contradictory answers.

Not testing in the field

A form that looks fine on a laptop can be slow or confusing on a basic phone with weak signal. Test with two or three real users before you roll out.

Changing the form without a plan

Renaming a field mid-programme can break your dashboard. Version your form, write down changes and update the report at the same time.
