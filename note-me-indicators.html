How to write a monitoring indicator people can actually measure

A good indicator says exactly what is counted, for whom, how often and where the number comes from. Here is how to write one.

In short

  • Every indicator needs a definition, a numerator and denominator, a source, a frequency and an owner.
  • Set a baseline and a target, or you cannot say whether a result is good.
  • Test the definition on last month's data: if two people get different answers, it is not finished.

An indicator that nobody can measure is just a wish. Frameworks often contain indicators like "improved community health" that sound right but cannot be counted the same way twice. Before an indicator goes into a logframe or a report, it should answer five questions.

The anatomy of an indicator

PartQuestion it answersExample
DefinitionWhat exactly is counted?Children under 1 who received the third dose of pentavalent vaccine
Numerator and denominatorWhat sits on top and underneath?Doses given / target population for the period
SourceWhere does the number come from?Facility immunisation register and monthly summary
FrequencyHow often is it collected and reported?Monthly, reviewed quarterly
Responsible personWho collects it and who checks it?Facility in-charge collects; data officer checks

Weak versus strong

  • Weak: Improved immunisation coverage
  • Strong: Percentage of children under 1 in the district who received Penta 3, reported monthly from facility registers
  • Weak: Staff trained
  • Strong: Number of facility staff who attended all three days of data quality training and scored 70% or more on the post-test

Set a baseline and a target

Without a starting point you cannot show change. Without a target you cannot say whether the result is good. Record where the baseline came from and the year, so it can be checked later.

Output or outcome?

Outputs are what the programme delivers, such as people trained. Outcomes are the changes that follow, such as reports arriving on time. Donors usually want both. Be careful not to report an output as if it proves an outcome.

Keep the list short

Every indicator costs time to collect, check and report. If nobody will use a number to make a decision, remove it. Five indicators people trust are worth more than twenty nobody reads.

Test it with real data

Tip

Write the full definition in an indicator reference sheet, one page per indicator, and share it with everyone who collects or reports the number.
