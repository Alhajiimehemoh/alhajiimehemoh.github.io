How to write a monitoring indicator people can actually measure

A good indicator says what is counted, for whom, how often and where the number comes from.

21 October 2026 · M&E · 1 min read

An indicator that nobody can measure is just a wish. Before you add one to a framework, check that it answers these questions.

The five parts

  • Definition: exactly what is counted, for example children under one who received the third dose
  • Numerator and denominator: if it is a percentage, say what sits on top and what sits underneath
  • Source: the register, form or survey the number comes from
  • Frequency: how often it is collected and reported
  • Responsible person: who collects it and who checks it

Set a baseline and a target

Without a starting point you cannot show change. Without a target you cannot say whether the result is good.

Keep the list short

Every indicator costs time to collect. If nobody will use a number in a decision, remove it.

Test with real data

Try the indicator on last month's data. If two people calculate different answers, the definition is not clear enough yet.
