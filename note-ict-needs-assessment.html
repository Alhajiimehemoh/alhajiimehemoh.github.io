How to run a quick ICT needs assessment

Before buying laptops or software, find out what your team actually needs and what already works. A short assessment you can run in a week.

Alhaji Ibrahim Mehemoh

Published 21 October 2026Updated 22 October 20262 min read

On this page

  1. Start with the work, not the equipment
  2. Take stock of what you have
  3. Set priorities
  4. Make a short plan
  5. Review every three months

Share

WhatsApp

In short

  • Start from the work and its problems, not from equipment.
  • Take stock of devices, internet, power, software, data and skills before you spend.
  • Fix the cheap, high-impact problems first, then plan the expensive ones.

Organisations often buy laptops, software or a new internet package first and ask questions later. Six months on, the laptops sit unused, the software does not fit the work and the real problem, perhaps lost files or late reports, is still there. A short needs assessment avoids that waste.

Start with the work, not the equipment

Sit with each team for half an hour and ask three questions:

  1. What are the main tasks you do every week?
  2. What slows you down or goes wrong most often?
  3. What would you stop doing if technology worked well?

Typical answers include late reports, lost files, slow or unreliable internet, entering the same data twice, or nobody knowing how to fix the printer.

Take stock of what you have

AreaWhat to record
DevicesEvery computer, phone and printer: age, condition, who uses it
Internet and powerHow reliable, how fast, what it costs per month, backup power
SoftwareWhat is licensed, what is actually used, what is out of date
DataWhere files live, who can access them, whether they are backed up
SecurityAntivirus, passwords, updates, shared accounts
SkillsWhat staff can do now and what they need to learn
Tip

Record devices in an asset register as you go. You will need it anyway, and it makes the assessment twice as useful.

Set priorities

Rank every problem by how much it costs the organisation in time, money or risk, and by how much it would cost to fix.

Quick wins often include setting up backups, fixing shared passwords, a short training session, a better form or a cheap Wi-Fi extender. They build trust and free up time for the bigger changes.

Make a short plan

Write one page with a simple table: what to do, why, who is responsible, the cost and the date. Keep it realistic. Three things done well beat ten things started.

ActionWhyOwnerCostBy when
Weekly backup to external driveFiles are on one laptop onlyOffice leadOne driveEnd of month
Two-step verification on emailShared passwords are a riskIT supportFreeTwo weeks
Excel training for programme staffReports take days to prepareData officerStaff timeNext quarter

Review every three months

Check what was done, what changed and what is next. Needs change as programmes grow, so the assessment should be a living document, not a one-off report.
