How to check EPI data quality before you report

Six quick checks that catch most errors in monthly immunisation data before they reach a dashboard.

21 October 2026 · Health data · 1 min read

Immunisation data passes through many hands before it becomes a coverage figure. A few minutes of checking at the start saves hours of explaining later. These are the checks I run on a monthly EPI export.

1. Completeness

List every facility that should report and tick off the ones that did. A missing facility is the most common reason coverage looks low. Also look for blank required fields. A blank is not the same as zero.

2. Timeliness

Note which reports arrived late and by how many days. Late reports often get entered in a rush, so they deserve a closer look.

3. Totals that add up

Check that the doses by age group add up to the facility total. If they do not, one of the numbers is wrong and the report should go back to the facility.

4. Impossible or unlikely values

Sort each indicator from highest to lowest and read the top and bottom rows. A facility that vaccinated far more children than its target population needs a conversation, not a chart.

5. Duplicates

Check for repeated submissions from the same facility and month. Keep the latest valid one and note what you removed.

6. Compare with last month

A sudden jump or drop can be real, but it is more often an entry error or a change in who reported. Write down the reason next to the number.

Keep a log

Record every correction: what changed, who confirmed it and when. A simple sheet is enough. It makes your report defensible when a donor or the DHMT asks questions.

I turned these into a one-page checklist you can print. You can download it from the resources page.
